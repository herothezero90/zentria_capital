When a business approaches the capital markets for the first time, the quality of preparation usually matters more than the strength of the underlying opportunity. Funders see hundreds of proposals; the ones that move forward quickly share a few common traits.

## Know your numbers cold

Before any introduction, you should be able to speak confidently to your revenue history, margins, existing debt obligations and the specific use of the capital you're seeking. Vague answers at this stage cost credibility that is hard to win back.

## Be clear on the structure you want

Debt, equity and joint venture structures each carry different obligations and different kinds of partners. You don't need to have the final answer — that's part of what an adviser is for — but you should understand the trade-offs.

## Prepare for the questions you'd rather not answer

Every business has weaknesses. Funders know this, and they're less interested in whether weaknesses exist than in whether management understands them and has a plan.

A well-prepared first conversation shortens every subsequent step of the process.
