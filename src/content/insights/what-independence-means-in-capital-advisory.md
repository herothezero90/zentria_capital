"Independent" is one of the most used — and least examined — words in capital advisory. It's worth examining, because the difference shows up in outcomes.

## The question behind the introduction

An adviser tied to a single funder starts from "how do I make this deal fit my capital?" An independent adviser starts from "which capital fits this deal?" The two questions lead to very different introductions.

## Alignment you can verify

Ask any prospective adviser how they are compensated, whether they hold placement arrangements with specific funds, and what happens to their fee if you walk away from a term sheet. Clear answers are a good sign. Hesitation is information too.

## Independence is a discipline, not a label

Staying independent means turning down arrangements that would be easier and more profitable. The advisers who do it consistently tend to be the ones who measure themselves on the quality of the match, not the speed of the close.
